Velocity is the points (or cards, if we did not use points) in Done at the end of the sprint.
Our cards have no story points, so velocity is measured in cards: the number of Sprint 2 cards in Done at the end of the sprint. Cards are not all the same size, so this is a rough measure, and it is only comparable between our own sprints.

## What happened

Most of the sprint's work stayed in In progress or In review, and very few cards reached Done while the sprint was running. The stand-up log shows this. At stand-up 1 everyone was "just started". At stand-up 2 only Omar had pushed work, and Yousif, Rayyan and Layla had committed only locally. At stand-up 3 Rayyan and Layla had not started, and at stand-up 4 Layla still had nothing committed. Work was finalised on the last day, and at stand-up 5 the team was still finishing the sequence diagram and waiting on reviews.

## Why

Exams and assignments in the team's other courses overlapped, and members had to put one aside for the other. Sprint 1 impediment 4 predicted this, and it came back in Sprint 2. Smaller causes were the diagrams that had to be done in sequence and the stand-up logs that were committed late.

## Reading the numbers

Velocity measured at the sprint end understates how much work was done, because most of it reached Done in the last days. The burndown for this sprint should therefore stay close to flat and then drop sharply at the end, instead of following the ideal line. Sprint 1 had the same pattern: all five package pull requests were merged on its last day.

## For Sprint 3

* Plan a lighter load for the weeks with exams and assignments, and set the commitment after checking everyone's deadlines.
* Commit small pieces during the sprint instead of keeping work local.
* Review pull requests within a day, so work doesn't pile up in In review.
* Commit each stand-up log and proof on the same day.
